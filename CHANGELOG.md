## 1.0.0-beta.68
- **LoRa Stumps stay in #lora-mesh on a chain's Stump without a radio** (the kit bench: a chain of an S3-CAM with a radio and a WROVER without, and two WROVERs on LoRa in no chain). ## 1.0.0-beta.57
- **The WROVER made a working Stump, and the Stump image** (everything found on the bench with three Freenove ESP32-WROVERs, a CAM and two Heltecs: a Stump that's slow, a chain joined the wrong way, a Provisioner that certified what didn't work). In order of what the bench turned up:
- **The board step knows a WROVER.** Its one USB port goes through a CH340 bridge, like a Heltec's, so the USB ids can't tell them apart: it was shown as "likely the Heltec V3" with a "doesn't look like a CAM" warning. For a Stump board on a bridge port the Provisioner now asks the chip (esptool, read-only): an original ESP32 is a WROVER and goes straight on; an ESP32-S3 behind a bridge is a Heltec and it warns before going on. The labels say "Stump board" (was "CAM").
- **The MicroPython image is picked with the other questions** (section 3), so the unattended part has nothing left to ask; the first WROVER run stopped in section 4 for it. When one image is found nearby, Enter takes it (it used to skip it: "blank to skip"); `s` skips. Without an image, nothing is put on the board: sections 4-7 used to push the settings and packs onto a board with no Stump on it, and fail. The "no image" help names the right download for each board.
- **No saved settings: fresh answers, or the board's own.** A flashed board is always asked the setup questions (flashing erases its settings: the second WROVER run kept "nothing"); an update chooses between answering them and keeping the board's own settings. Settings saved by earlier runs are never reused: they carried old choices into a new board without saying so (a WROVER kept joining a hotspot after the router way was chosen).

- **Each Stump its own hotspot name, for its Heltec.** Every Stump on the default name broadcasts "LaBuche-Stump.web.app", so a Heltec told to join "this Stump's hotspot" could join another Stump's (found with three WROVERs and a CAM near each other). With a Heltec on its own hotspot, a Stump on the default name is now asked for a name of its own; the hotspot question says *n* names it; the Heltec's suggested address is in this Stump's range (192.168.<chain number>.2, it was always .4.2); and the name shown is the one broadcast (it showed "<node name>-192.168.4.1" while the board broadcast the default).
- **Connecting to the Heltec never holds the Stump.** With its Heltec off or not set up yet, every reconnect froze the Stump 4 to 20 s (TCP's own give-up: the ESP32's MicroPython ignores `settimeout()` on `connect()`), and nothing else ran meanwhile: no page, no captive portal; a page could stop after its headers. Found on a WROVER by the watchdog's new line ("busy for 20516 ms: connecting to the Heltec at 192.168.5.3"). The connect now starts without blocking, the poll loop finishes it, and it gives up after 3 s.
- **A port held by another program is said as such.** A `screen` left running on the board's port made the flash fail with "try a different USB cable... hold BOOT". The Provisioner now names who holds the port (`lsof`: "screen (pid 812)"), says how to close it, and before section 4 touches the board, waits for it to be free. The upload stops at the first file that finds the port busy (it went on through 100 files, the settings and the packs, each failing the same way).
- **A chain number is never that of the Stump it joins.** Joining "LaBuche1 192.168.5.1" (number 5) with number 5 too gave both the same hotspot address. The number is read from the uplink's hotspot name: refused if the same, and the suggested one is the next.
- **The Heltec's address on its Stump's hotspot is .200**, past what the hotspot hands out to phones (ESP-IDF's DHCP server: .2 to .101). It was .2, the first visitor's address too: a Mac on a WROVER's hotspot got 192.168.6.2, its Heltec's address. The Heltec's own setup suggests 192.168.<the number in the hotspot's name>.200.
- **"Find a CAM's addresses" and the setup's summary name the Wi-Fi it joined** (the summary: "Via the chain http://192.168.4.3/ on LaBuche-Stump.web.app"), and for a Stump in a chain says that's the hotspot of the Stump above, not the home Wi-Fi (a laptop at home couldn't open its "LAN IP" 192.168.4.3).
- **The board is running when the Provisioner is done.** Its last checks (`--diag`, finding the address, also `--get-ip`) ask over USB with mpremote, which stops the Stump, and it stayed stopped: a "certified" board served no page and no captive portal until someone pressed EN. It's restarted after them.
- **The chat bridge says which of its steps held the Stump** (a message out, to whom; the chain's Stumps; the radio's turn; a memory clean-up), and the time a memory clean-up and a chain-secret signature take are said when slow (`[mem] ...`, `[chain secret] ...`, at most once a minute). A WROVER was "busy for 3203 ms: the chat bridge (mesh sends)", by which part not said.
- **A board Stump joins its chain over the home Wi-Fi**, like a computer Stump. A CAM or a WROVER that's a chain member used its one Wi-Fi connection for the hotspot of the Stump before it, so it had no home address and couldn't be opened from the home network. With `CHAIN_PARENT_IP` (the first Stump's address on the home Wi-Fi) it stays on `WIFI_SSID` (home address, clock, internet) and links to the first Stump there; its own hotspot still serves visitors, its Heltec and Stumps below it. When the router moves the first Stump, it finds it again on that network (discovery broadcast, then `/chain.json`: root, same chain, taking links there), never leaving it to scan hotspots. The Provisioner offers it in the chain question ("Over the Wi-Fi it joins"), finds the first Stump from the computer, and asks for the chain secret (needed). `/chain.json` `uplink.over_lan`. docs/CHAIN.md.
- **A board set up for the router stays on the router.** Off it (not there at boot, or dropped), it joins it again every 30 s and nothing else: it used to give up on the router after a minute and hunt for Stump hotspots, and joined the first Stump's (a WROVER with the router settings came up on "LaBuche-Stump.web.app" with no home address). The chip's own reconnects are on before the first join.
- **A board in a chain, and its Heltec, on the router by default** (the Provisioner): "Over the router (recommended): like your other Stumps" is the chain question's first choice (the router's Wi-Fi asked there if it wasn't), and the Heltec's first choice is the router too, at a fixed address past the router's DHCP range (.222 on this computer's network). The hotspot ways stay, as second choices.
- **The setup's last check reaches the board the way people will.** After the USB checks ("USB CHECKS PASSED", no longer "certified" on their own), the Provisioner opens the board's page from this computer at its router address and checks its Heltec is connected (`/hw.json`), waiting for it to come up. Certified only if both: a board on another Stump's hotspot, a computer on another network, no answer, another Stump at that address or a Heltec not connected each fail, saying which. The summary's "Open it at" is that address. (A WROVER was "certified" with an address that worked from nowhere the computer was.)
- **Room to work in** (`urns/mem.headroom`). MicroPython's ESP32 port grows its heap only when an allocation fails *after* a full collection, so the heap stays just big enough for what's live and every few hundred KB of churn (a page or two) costs a full collection, ~2 s on a WROVER, however few the code asks for. At boot the Stump allocates and frees 2 MB once, so the port adds that much heap and the churn has room before the next collection. The boot log says the heap's size (`[mem] at boot: ...`, `[mem] with room to work in: ...`).
- **Memory clean-ups only when memory is low** (`urns/mem.py`). The code forced a full garbage collection at 65 places, several per packet (a chain secret's signature and check, proofs, announces) and after every DNS answer of the captive portal: from boards without PSRAM. On a WROVER one takes ~2 s ("a memory clean-up took 2040 ms (839 KB in use)"), so each packet and each phone's portal check froze the Stump for seconds. Now a collection runs only under 25% (or 96 KB) free; MicroPython still collects by itself when the heap is full. The /admin plugin RAM figure no longer forces one.
- **The watchdog names what held the Stump.** "the Stump was busy for N ms" now ends with what it had started last (a web page and its address, a packet and the interface it came on, a plugin's callback or timer, connecting to the Heltec, an LXMF send, the chat bridge, the chain watch, a propagation stamp check), and says each long one (at most one line per 10 s), not only a new record. Found on a WROVER busy 15 s, by what not said.
- `--diag`: a chained Stump's hotspot address is 192.168.<its number>.1 (it always said 192.168.4.1).
